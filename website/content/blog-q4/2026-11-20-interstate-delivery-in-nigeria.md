**Interstate delivery in Nigeria** lets a seller in Lagos sell to a customer in Kano, or a seller in Aba reach buyers in Abuja and Port Harcourt. To do it well, you need four things: packaging that survives a long journey, a delivery timeline you can actually keep, a price that covers every cost, and a way to tell the customer where their parcel is.

Get those right and interstate orders can grow your business well beyond your city. Get them wrong and you'll spend your evenings answering "where is my order?" and replacing broken items. This guide covers each part, with checklists you can use from your next order.

## Why sell outside your state?

Many sellers stay local because interstate feels risky. But some of your most loyal customers may be in other states, especially if you sell things that are hard to find there.

- **A fabric seller in Aba or Kano** can supply customers who can't get the same quality locally.
- **A gadget seller near Computer Village** can reach buyers across the country.
- **A skincare brand in Abuja** can build a following in Port Harcourt, Enugu and beyond.

The key is to treat interstate orders as their own kind of order, with their own packing, timeline and price.

## How interstate delivery in Nigeria works

Most interstate parcels go through a few stages:

1. **Pickup or drop-off.** A rider collects from you, or you take it to a hub or terminal.
2. **Sorting.** The parcel is grouped with others going the same way.
3. **Line haul.** It travels between cities by road, and sometimes by air for faster services.
4. **Arrival and sorting** at the destination city.
5. **Last-mile delivery** to the customer's door, or collection at a pickup point.

Each handover is a point where delays or damage can happen. That's why packing and tracking matter so much more than for same-city orders.

:::note Door delivery or pickup point?
Some services deliver to the door, others ask the customer to collect from a branch or terminal. Tell the customer which one before they pay.
:::

## Packing for a long journey

Your parcel may be stacked under heavier boxes, loaded and unloaded several times, and exposed to heat or rain. Pack for the worst trip, not the best.

### General packing rules

- **Use a sturdy outer layer.** A strong carton or a thick waterproof courier bag, not a thin nylon.
- **Fill empty space.** Use paper, bubble wrap or old cartons so nothing moves when you shake it.
- **Seal every seam** with good tape, in an H pattern on boxes.
- **Waterproof the inside.** Put fabric, paper products and electronics in a sealed nylon first.
- **Label clearly** with the customer's name, phone number, full address and a landmark.

### Packing by product type

**Clothes and fabric.** Fold neatly, place in a sealed nylon to keep dry, then in a courier bag. Add a thank-you card on top.

**Bags and shoes.** Stuff with paper to keep the shape. Wrap each item, then box it so it doesn't get crushed.

**Skincare and liquids.** Tape lids shut, wrap each bottle in bubble wrap, and put them in a sealed nylon in case of leaks. Stand bottles upright in a box with padding.

**Phones and gadgets.** Keep the original box if possible, wrap it in bubble wrap, then put it in a bigger carton with padding on all sides. Don't write "phone" on the outside.

**Food.** Only send items that stay safe without cooling for the whole journey. Dry foods like chin chin, cashew or spices travel better than fresh items. Seal well and label the packing date.

:::checklist Before you seal the box
The right item, size and colour is inside
Item is wrapped and cannot move when shaken
Liquids are taped and sealed in nylon
Invoice or packing slip is inside
Customer name, phone and full address are on the label
Your shop name and phone are on the label
You have taken a photo of the packed item and label
:::

:::tip Take a photo every time
A quick photo of the item before sealing, and of the sealed parcel with its label, gives you proof if a customer or delivery partner raises a problem.
:::

## Timelines: what to promise customers

Interstate delivery takes longer than same-city delivery, and it's less predictable. Delays can come from road conditions, weather, public holidays, festive rush periods and how often your partner runs a route.

The safest approach:

- **Ask your delivery partner** for typical timelines on the routes you use most.
- **Test a few parcels** and note how long they actually take.
- **Promise a range, not a single day.** "3 to 5 working days" is easier to keep than "Wednesday".
- **Count working days.** Weekends and public holidays may not count.
- **Add buffer in busy periods** like Black Friday and December.

### Your dispatch cut-off

Decide a daily cut-off time. For example: "Orders paid before 12 noon are dispatched the same day. Orders after 12 go out the next working day." Put it on your store and in your WhatsApp replies so customers know.

:::warn Don't overpromise
Promising "next day to anywhere" to win an order, then missing it, does more harm than giving an honest longer timeline. Customers forgive slow. They don't forgive surprises.
:::

## Costs to plan for

Interstate delivery costs more than same-city delivery, and the price can depend on several things. Don't quote from memory. Get current prices from your delivery partner and build every cost into your plan.

### What usually affects the delivery price

- **Distance and route** between the two cities
- **Weight and size** of the parcel
- **Speed of service**: standard road, express, or air
- **Door delivery versus pickup point**
- **Special handling** for fragile or high-value items

### Costs sellers often forget

- **Packaging.** Stronger boxes, bubble wrap and tape cost more than a nylon.
- **Getting the parcel to the hub** if pickup isn't included.
- **Insurance or declared value cover** for expensive items, if your partner offers it.
- **Failed deliveries.** If the customer can't be reached, the parcel may come back and you may pay for the return.
- **Replacements** when something is damaged.

### Who pays?

Most sellers charge interstate delivery to the customer. The cleanest way is to **show the delivery price at checkout**, so the customer sees the total before paying. If you want to offer free interstate delivery, add the average cost into your product prices or set a minimum order value for it.

**Use bundles for interstate buyers.** If a customer in Kano pays for delivery anyway, suggest one more item: "Since delivery is the same for two, would you like the matching scarf?" It makes the delivery fee feel more worth it.

## Payment for interstate orders

Pay on delivery is harder across states, because cash has to travel back to you and returns cost more. Many sellers ask interstate customers to pay before dispatch.

To make that easier for new customers:

- **Share a checkout link** so they can pay by card, bank transfer or USSD and get a receipt.
- **Show reviews and photos** from past customers in other states.
- **Send a photo** of the packed parcel and the waybill or tracking number once it ships.

## Tracking and keeping customers updated

A customer who knows where their parcel is will wait patiently. A customer who hears nothing will message you five times.

### What to share

1. **When you dispatch:** "Your order has been sent. Here's your tracking number."
2. **If there's a delay:** tell them before they ask, with a new expected date.
3. **When it's out for delivery:** "Please keep your phone close, the rider will call."
4. **After delivery:** "Did your order arrive well?"

### Sample dispatch message

> Hello Musa, your order has left Lagos today and should reach you in Kano in 3 to 5 working days. Your tracking number is [number]. The rider will call you on this number before delivery. Thank you for shopping with us.

### Handling problems

Even with good packing, things go wrong sometimes.

- **Delayed parcel.** Check tracking, contact your partner, then update the customer with honest information.
- **Damaged item.** Ask the customer for photos straight away, compare with your packing photos, and follow your partner's claims process.
- **Customer unreachable.** Ask your partner how many attempts they make and how long they hold the parcel. Try to reach the customer yourself on WhatsApp.
- **Wrong item sent.** Apologise, arrange a swap, and decide who covers return delivery based on your returns policy.

Having a written return and refund policy makes these conversations much easier, because the customer knows the rules before they buy.
