If you want to know **how to sell on Instagram in Nigeria**, the short answer is this: switch to a business account, pick one clear thing to sell, post photos and Reels that show the product in real use, put your price where people can see it, reply to DMs quickly and give buyers a safe way to pay. Then deliver on time and ask happy customers to tag you.

That is the whole game. The rest of this guide breaks each part into steps you can follow from your phone, with Nigerian examples, so you spend less time posting into silence and more time packing orders.

## Why Instagram still works for Nigerian sellers

Instagram is where many Nigerian shoppers go to look before they buy. People scroll for outfits, hair, skincare, food trays, gadgets and home items, then send a DM to ask "how much?" It is visual, it is social, and buyers can see that a real person is behind the page.

It also has limits. Your posts only reach a slice of your followers, DMs pile up, and there is no built-in checkout for most Nigerian sellers. Knowing this from day one helps you set things up properly instead of hoping the algorithm does the work.

### Who does well on Instagram

Many sellers find Instagram works best for products that look good in pictures and video:

- Fashion: ready-to-wear, ankara pieces, bags, shoes and jewellery
- Beauty: skincare, wigs, lashes, nails and perfume oils
- Food: small chops, cakes, snack boxes and drinks
- Home: throw pillows, candles, kitchen items and décor
- Gadgets: phone accessories, earbuds and smart home items

If your product is hard to show visually, you can still sell, but you will lean more on captions, explainer Reels and customer reviews.

## Step 1: set up an Instagram account that looks like a real shop

First impressions decide whether someone follows, scrolls past or reports you as a scam. Treat your profile like a shop front on a busy Lagos street.

:::steps Set up your profile in one sitting
Switch to a professional account | In settings, change to a business or creator account so you get insights and contact buttons.
Choose a clear username | Use your brand name, short and easy to spell, without random numbers or underscores.
Write a useful bio | Say what you sell, where you deliver and how to order, in two or three short lines.
Add contact buttons | Add your WhatsApp number, email and location so buyers have more than one way to reach you.
Put a link in your bio | Link to a store page where people can see prices and pay without waiting for a DM reply.
:::

### What a good bio looks like

Here is an example bio for a fictional bag seller:

> Everyday leather bags for working women 👜
> Lagos delivery in 24 hours. Nationwide 2 to 4 days.
> Prices and order 👇

Notice what it does. It names the product, the customer and the delivery promise, then points to the link. No long story, no "DM for price" with nothing else.

### Highlights that build trust

Use Story Highlights as your mini website. Good highlights for Nigerian sellers include:

- **Prices** or "Shop" with your current range
- **Reviews** with screenshots of happy customer chats (blur their names)
- **Delivery** showing packed orders and riders picking up
- **How to order** in three simple slides
- **FAQs** covering sizes, returns and payment

## Step 2: choose what to sell and who it is for

Pages that try to sell everything usually sell very little. Pick one main category and one type of customer, at least at the start.

Ask yourself three questions:

1. Who is my buyer? For example, "young professionals in Lekki and Yaba" or "mums in Abuja buying school items".
2. What problem does my product solve for them? Look good at work, save time, feel confident, feed guests.
3. Can I get this product reliably? If your supplier disappears every two weeks, your page will go quiet too.

If you do not have stock yet, you do not need to buy a big load before you start. You can [find checked suppliers on the marketplace](/marketplace/) and choose to dropship (the supplier ships with your shop's label) or buy wholesale once you see what sells.

:::tip Start narrow, then widen
Choose 10 to 20 products in one category for your first month. Once you know what people ask about most, add related items. A wig seller can add edge control and bonnets later.
:::

## Step 3: post content that makes people ask "how much?"

On Instagram, content is your shop window and your salesperson. The aim is not to be famous. The aim is to make the right people stop, trust you and ask how to buy.

### The four types of post that sell

Rotate between these so your page does not feel like an endless price list:

- **Product in use:** someone wearing the dress, using the blender, opening the cake box. Real hands and real rooms beat plain studio shots.
- **Proof:** customer reviews, unboxing clips, "packing today's orders" videos.
- **Education:** how to style it, how to care for it, size guides, "3 ways to wear one scarf".
- **Behind the scenes:** you sourcing at Balogun, fabric arriving, the tailor at work. People buy from people.

### Reels or photos?

Reels tend to reach people who do not follow you yet, so use them to find new buyers. Photo carousels are great for detail: front, back, close-up of the stitching, size label, price. Many sellers do well with three to five Reels a week plus a few carousels.

Keep Reels short and start with the product, not your logo. The first two seconds should show the thing being worn, opened or used.

### Always show the price

"DM for price" frustrates buyers and many simply scroll away. If you worry about competitors copying your prices, remember that buyers will find out anyway. Showing **₦18,500** in the caption or on the image saves you dozens of DMs from people who were never going to buy at that price.

### Write captions that move people to order

A good caption answers the questions a buyer would ask before paying. Keep it short, clear and easy to read on a phone.

Use this simple structure:

1. A hook line about the benefit: "The bag that fits your laptop and still looks soft."
2. Two or three key details: size, material, colours, what is included.
3. Price and delivery: "₦24,000. Lagos delivery tomorrow."
4. How to order: "Tap the link in bio to order or send us a DM."

For more on this, our guide on writing product descriptions goes deeper with before and after examples. Keep a notes file of captions that worked so you can reuse the structure.

#### Hashtags and location

Add a few relevant hashtags (your product, your city, your niche) rather than thirty random ones. Tag your location, for example Lagos or Abuja, so local buyers see you when they browse places.

## Step 4: handle DMs fast and turn chats into orders

This is where most Instagram sales are won or lost. A buyer who asks "how much?" at 9pm and hears nothing until noon has probably bought from someone else.

:::checklist A good DM reply includes
Greeting and the buyer's name if you have it
Price and whether the item is in stock
Size or colour options
Delivery fee and how long it takes to their area
A link to pay or a clear next step
:::

### Save time with quick replies

Instagram lets you save quick replies for common questions. Set up replies for price, delivery areas, sizes and how to pay. Keep them friendly, not robotic.

### Move serious buyers to checkout

Long back-and-forth chats about account numbers waste time and invite mistakes. Once a buyer is ready, send them a link to the product page where they can pick options, see the delivery price and pay. You then see a clean order instead of a screenshot you have to match to a bank alert.

## Step 6: deliver well, then ask for reviews

Delivery is part of your Instagram marketing. A fast, neat delivery leads to tags, reviews and repeat orders. A late one leads to angry comments.

Practical tips:

- Tell buyers the delivery price before they pay, not after.
- Pack neatly with your shop name on the package or a thank-you card.
- Send a message when the order leaves and when it arrives.
- Two or three days later, ask: "Did everything arrive well? We'd love a quick photo or review."

Repost those reviews to your Stories and save the best ones to your Reviews highlight. Over time this becomes your strongest sales tool.

## Mistakes that slow Instagram sales

Most pages that struggle are making one or more of these mistakes:

- **Buying followers.** Fake followers do not buy and they make your engagement look weak.
- **Posting only flyers.** Busy graphics with prices and phone numbers look like ads. Show the real product.
- **Inconsistent posting.** Posting ten times in one week, then disappearing for a month, confuses both buyers and Instagram.
- **Hiding prices.** As above, it costs you more than it protects you.
- **Ignoring comments.** Reply to every genuine comment. It shows the page is alive.
- **No way to order outside DMs.** If the only way to buy is to chat, you limit how many orders you can handle.

## Growing beyond your first sales

Once orders come in steadily, look for ways to grow without burning out.

### Reuse content across platforms

The same Reel can go on TikTok, Facebook and your WhatsApp Status. Selling on several channels from one product list saves hours. Shopinbos lets you [sell across Instagram, TikTok, Facebook, WhatsApp and your own store](/features/social-selling/) while keeping orders and stock in one place.

### Work with micro influencers

Small creators with loyal local audiences can be more useful than big names. Offer a product in exchange for honest content, or agree a fee, and track orders with a simple discount code.

### Run small ads on your best posts

When a post is already getting saves and DMs, boosting it with a small daily budget can reach more people like your buyers. Start small, watch what happens to DMs and orders, and only keep spending on what works.
