A **WhatsApp broadcast message for business** is one message you send to many customers at once, where each person receives it as a private chat from you. To use it well, only message people who have saved your number and agreed to hear from you, keep messages short and useful, send them sparingly, and always give an easy way to reply or opt out.

Done right, broadcasts are one of the cheapest ways to bring back past customers and announce new stock. Done badly, they get you muted, blocked or reported. This guide covers how broadcasts work, the rules to follow, how to grow an opt-in list and 10 templates you can copy and adapt today.

## How WhatsApp broadcast lists work

In the WhatsApp Business app, a broadcast list is a saved group of contacts. When you send a message to the list, each person gets it as a normal one-to-one chat. They cannot see who else received it, and if they reply, only you see the reply.

This is different from a WhatsApp group, where everyone sees everyone else's messages and numbers. Broadcasts feel more personal and protect your customers' privacy.

### The rule most sellers miss

**Only people who have saved your number in their phone will receive your broadcast.** If a customer has not saved you, your message simply does not reach them. That is why getting customers to save your number is step one of any broadcast plan.

### Limits change, so check the app

WhatsApp sets limits on how many contacts can be in one broadcast list and has been adjusting how many broadcasts business accounts can send. These rules change from time to time, so check the current limits inside the app before you plan big campaigns.

If you outgrow the app, the WhatsApp Business Platform (the API) lets businesses send approved message templates at scale, with stricter opt-in rules. Most small sellers start with the app.

## How to create a broadcast list

:::steps Set up your first broadcast
Open the WhatsApp Business app | Go to the Chats tab and open the menu.
Choose New broadcast | Tap the option for a new broadcast or broadcast lists.
Select contacts | Pick the customers who have agreed to hear from you.
Name your list | Use names like "Past buyers", "Lagos customers" or "Wig lovers" so you can target later.
Write and send | Type your message, add a photo if it helps, then send.
:::

The exact menu names can differ between Android and iPhone and between app updates, but broadcasts live in the Chats menu.

### Use more than one list

Do not put everyone in one list. Split by what they buy or where they are, for example:

- Past buyers vs new enquiries
- Lagos vs other states
- Fashion buyers vs beauty buyers
- Wholesale buyers vs retail buyers

Targeted messages feel more relevant and get fewer blocks.

## The rules: how to broadcast without getting blocked

Every time someone blocks or reports your number, it can hurt your account. WhatsApp may restrict numbers that get many reports. Follow these rules to stay safe.

:::checklist Broadcast rules to follow
Only message people who opted in to hear from you
Introduce yourself if it has been a while
Keep messages short and easy to read on a phone
Send no more than one or two broadcasts a week to the same list
Make every message useful: new stock, restock, offer, reminder
Always say how to stop receiving messages
Remove people who ask to stop, straight away
:::

### What counts as opt-in

Opt-in means the customer clearly agreed to receive messages from you. Good ways to get it:

- They bought from you and you asked, "Can I add you to our new arrivals list?" and they said yes
- They messaged you first and asked to be told when something is restocked
- They tapped a "Join our WhatsApp list" link on your store, bio or packaging
- They filled a short form or replied with a keyword like "YES"

Buying a list of numbers or adding everyone from a group is not opt-in. It is the quickest way to get blocked.

:::warn Do not buy or scrape numbers
Messaging people who never agreed to hear from you leads to reports and can get your number restricted. It also damages trust in your brand.
:::

## How to grow your opt-in list

A broadcast is only as good as the list behind it. Build yours slowly with real customers.

### Ask every buyer

After each order, send: "Thank you for your order! Please save this number as [Shop name] so you get our restock alerts. Reply STOP any time if you don't want them."

### Add a reason to join

People save numbers when there is something in it for them:

- First look at new stock
- Restock alerts for sold-out items
- Subscriber-only discounts
- Early access to sales before Christmas or Black Friday

### Put the link everywhere

Share your WhatsApp link in your Instagram and TikTok bios, on your store, in your email signature and on a small card inside every package. ## 10 WhatsApp broadcast templates you can copy

Replace the words in brackets with your own details. Keep the tone warm and personal. These examples use a fictional shop, so adjust them to fit your products.

### 1. Welcome message

> Hi [Name] 👋 Welcome to [Shop name]! Thanks for saving our number. We'll send you new arrivals and offers once or twice a week, no spam. Reply STOP any time to opt out.

### 2. New arrivals

> New in at [Shop name] ✨
> [Product name], [key detail], ₦[price].
> Only [number] pieces in this batch.
> Order here: [link]
> Reply STOP to opt out.

### 3. Restock alert

> Good news, [Name]! The [product name] you asked about is back in stock. Sizes [list]. ₦[price]. Order before it goes again: [link]

### 4. Limited-time offer

> This weekend only 🎉 Get [discount] off all [category] at [Shop name]. Offer ends Sunday 9pm. Shop now: [link]
> Reply STOP to stop these messages.

### 5. Payday reminder

> Happy month end! 💸 Your favourite [product] is waiting. Pay by card, transfer or USSD, or choose pay on delivery in Lagos, Abuja, Ibadan and Port Harcourt. Order: [link]

### 6. Order follow-up and review request

> Hi [Name], we hope your [product] arrived well 😊 Could you reply with a quick photo or one line about how you like it? It helps other buyers trust us. Thank you!

### 7. Win-back message

> Hi [Name], it's been a while! We've added new [category] since your last order. Here's [discount] off your next one with code [CODE], valid till [date]. See what's new: [link]

### 8. Festive season

> Christmas orders are open 🎄 Order by [date] for delivery before the 24th in Lagos. Gift wrapping available. Shop gifts: [link]

### 9. Pidgin version

> How far [Name]! New [product] don land for [Shop name] 🔥 Na ₦[price]. E no go last, so make you order sharp sharp: [link]
> If you no want these messages again, reply STOP.

### 10. Event or pop-up invite

> We'll be at [event name], [location], on [date] from [time] 🛍️ Come say hi, try things on and get [offer] on the day. Can't make it? Shop online: [link]

:::tip Personalise where you can
Adding the customer's name and referring to what they bought before makes a broadcast feel like a real chat, not a mass advert. Even small touches help.
:::

### What makes these templates work

Look at what the templates have in common, so you can write your own:

- **One message, one goal.** Each template asks for one thing: order, reply, review or visit.
- **Clear price or offer.** Buyers can decide without asking.
- **A deadline where it fits.** "Ends Sunday 9pm" gives a reason to act now, but only use real deadlines.
- **A link.** Readers can act in one tap instead of starting a long chat.
- **An easy way out.** "Reply STOP" shows respect and lowers the chance of being reported.

Add one clear photo when you are showing a product. A good picture of the item often does more than extra words. Keep videos short so they load quickly on mobile data.

## When to send and how often

Timing affects whether people read your message or swipe it away.

- **Good times:** many sellers find mid-morning, lunchtime and early evening work well, but test with your own list.
- **Paydays:** month-end and the first few days of a new month often suit bigger purchases.
- **Avoid:** very early mornings, late nights and Sunday mornings.
- **Frequency:** once or twice a week is plenty for most lists. If people start muting or blocking you, slow down.

### Track what works

After each broadcast, note how many replies and orders you got. Over a few weeks you will see which types of message and which days work best for your customers.

## Handling replies after a broadcast

A good broadcast brings a wave of replies. Be ready.

- Have quick replies saved for price, sizes, delivery and payment
- Send a product link so buyers can pay without long back-and-forth
- Confirm payment before dispatch, never on screenshots alone
- Reply to opt-outs politely and remove them straight away
