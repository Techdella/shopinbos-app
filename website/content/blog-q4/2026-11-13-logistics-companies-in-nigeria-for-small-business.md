There are many **logistics companies in Nigeria for small business**, from bike dispatch riders who cover one part of Lagos to larger firms that move parcels between states. The right one for you is not the biggest or the cheapest. It's the one that reaches your customers, handles your kind of product with care, tells you where parcels are, and settles money and problems quickly.

This guide won't rank named companies, because a partner that works brilliantly for a Lekki shoe seller may be wrong for a cake maker in Abuja or a phone accessories seller shipping from Computer Village to Kano. Instead, you'll get a clear way to compare options, the questions to ask before you sign up, and a simple trial plan so you can choose with evidence.

## Why your delivery partner matters more than you think

For the customer, the delivery *is* your business. They don't see your supplier, your packaging table or your late nights. They see a rider who was either polite and on time, or rude and two days late.

A poor delivery partner costs you in ways that don't show up on the invoice:

- **Lost repeat orders.** A customer who waited three days for a "same-day" order rarely comes back.
- **Damaged goods.** You pay to replace the item and often the delivery again.
- **Missing cash.** With pay on delivery, slow or unclear cash remittance ties up your money.
- **Your time.** Every "where is my order?" message you have to chase is time not spent selling.

Choosing well at the start saves you all of this.

## The types of logistics options for small sellers

Before comparing companies, know which kind of service you need. Most small businesses use a mix.

### Independent dispatch riders

A rider or small team you call or message directly. Often flexible and good for same-day delivery within one city. The downside is that quality depends on one or two people, and tracking is usually "call the rider".

### City delivery companies

Businesses with a fleet of bikes or vans covering one or more cities. Usually offer booking, some tracking and set prices by zone. Good for regular intra-city orders.

### Interstate and nationwide carriers

Companies that move parcels between states, often through hubs or terminals. Timelines are longer and packaging matters more. Some deliver to the door, others to a pickup point.

### Park and bus waybill

Sending a parcel with a transport company from a motor park, for the customer to collect at the other end. Many sellers use it for bulky or cheaper items. It can work, but tracking and liability are usually limited, so be clear with the customer.

:::note Mix and match
Many sellers use one partner for same-day city orders and another for interstate. You don't need one company for everything.
:::

## What to look for in logistics companies in Nigeria for small business

When you compare logistics companies in Nigeria for small business, judge them on the same points every time. Here are the ones that matter most.

### Coverage

Can they reach where your customers actually are? Check your last 30 orders. If most are in Lagos Mainland with a few in Ibadan and Abuja, a partner that only covers Island is no use.

### Speed and reliability

"Same day" means nothing if it happens half the time. Ask what share of their deliveries arrive on the promised day, then test it yourself.

### Pricing you can predict

You need to show customers a delivery price at checkout. That's hard if your partner quotes a new price each time. Look for zone-based or weight-based pricing that's written down.

### Tracking and updates

At the very least, you should know when a parcel is picked up, out for delivery and delivered. Better still if the customer gets updates too.

### Handling of pay on delivery

If you offer POD, how do they collect cash? When is it paid to you? What proof do they give? Same-day remittance is much safer than weekly.

### Damage, loss and failed delivery

What happens if a parcel is damaged or lost? Who pays? What happens if the customer isn't home or refuses the item? Get these answers before you need them.

### Professional conduct

Riders represent your brand. Are they polite on the phone? Do they wait a reasonable time? Do they handle items with care?

## Questions to ask before you sign up

Copy these into your notes app and ask every partner you're considering. Their answers, and how clearly they give them, tell you a lot.

:::checklist Questions to ask a logistics company
Which areas and states do you cover, and which do you not?
How is your price worked out: by zone, distance, weight or size?
Do you send a written price list I can show at checkout?
What time do you pick up, and what is the cut-off for same-day delivery?
How do I and my customer track a parcel?
How many attempts do you make if the customer is not available?
What happens to a parcel after a failed delivery, and is return charged?
How do you collect pay on delivery cash, and when do you pay it to me?
What happens if an item is damaged or lost, and how do I make a claim?
Do you have restrictions on items such as food, glass, liquids or electronics?
Who do I contact when something goes wrong, and how fast do you respond?
:::

:::tip Listen for vague answers
"No wahala, we go handle am" is friendly, but it isn't an answer. A good partner can tell you clearly what happens when a delivery fails.
:::

## Matching the partner to your product

Different products need different handling. Think about what you sell before you choose.

- **Fashion and bags.** Light and hard to damage, but customers often want to try items on. Ask about waiting time and returns. See our [fashion sellers page](/solutions/fashion/).
- **Food and cakes.** Timing and temperature matter. You need a rider who goes straight to the customer, not one who does ten stops first. See [food sellers](/solutions/food/).
- **Beauty and skincare.** Liquids can leak and glass can break. Ask how parcels are stacked and carried.
- **Phones and gadgets.** High value, so ask about loss cover and proof of delivery. See [electronics sellers](/solutions/electronics/).
- **Furniture and large items.** You may need a van, not a bike, and two people to carry.

## How to test a partner before you commit

Don't move all your orders to a new partner on day one. Run a short trial.

:::steps Two-week delivery trial
Pick a small batch | Send 5 to 10 real orders through the new partner, ideally to different areas.
Write down the promise | Note the promised pickup and delivery time for each order.
Record what happened | Log actual pickup, delivery time, rider conduct and any problems.
Ask the customer | Send a short message after delivery: "Did your order arrive well? How was the rider?"
Test a problem | See how they handle a reschedule or a customer who is not home.
Compare and decide | Put the results side by side with your current option and choose on evidence.
:::

A simple table in your notes app is enough:

| Order | Area | Promised | Delivered | Condition | Customer feedback |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Example 1 | Yaba | Same day by 6pm | 4:30pm | Good | "Rider was polite" |
| Example 2 | Ajah | Same day by 6pm | Next day 11am | Good | "Had to call twice" |

After two weeks, the pattern is usually clear.

## Costs to plan for

The delivery fee is only part of the cost. When you compare partners, also think about:

- **Failed delivery and return fees.** Some partners charge to bring an item back.
- **Packaging.** Sturdier packaging for interstate costs more but saves replacements.
- **Cash remittance timing.** Money held for a week costs you restocking opportunities.
- **Your time.** A cheaper partner who needs constant chasing may cost you more in lost sales.

Decide who pays for delivery. Many sellers show the delivery price at checkout so the customer pays it, and offer free delivery only above a certain order value. That way delivery doesn't quietly eat your profit.

:::warn Don't hide delivery costs
Adding a delivery charge after the customer has agreed to a price feels like a trick, even when it isn't. Show it upfront.
:::

### Build a good relationship

Once you've chosen, treat your delivery partner like part of your team.

- **Have orders packed and labelled before pickup.** Riders waiting 30 minutes start to avoid you.
- **Give complete addresses and phone numbers**, plus a landmark ("opposite the Mobil filling station").
- **Tell customers to expect a call** from the rider so they pick up.
- **Share feedback early**, both good and bad.
- **Review every month.** Look at late deliveries, damage and complaints, and talk about them.
