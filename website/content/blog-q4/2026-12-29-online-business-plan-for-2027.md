A good online business plan for 2027 does not need to be 30 pages. For most Nigerian online sellers, it fits on one page: **what worked in 2026, three goals for 2027, and a 90-day plan with weekly actions.** The goal is a plan you will actually open in February, not a document that sits in a folder.

This guide walks you through it in four parts. First, a quick review of your year with the numbers that matter. Second, how to pick goals that are realistic. Third, a 90-day plan with a fill-in table. Fourth, how to review and adjust as you go. Set aside two quiet hours this week and you will start January with a clear head.

## Why a simple plan beats a big one

Many sellers start the year with big promises: "This year I will grow 10 times." By March, life happens, the naira moves, a supplier disappoints, and the promise fades.

A short plan works better because:

- **You can remember it.** Three goals are easy to keep in mind.
- **It is tied to actions,** not just wishes.
- **You review it often,** so you can adjust when things change.
- **It fits your real life,** with your job, family and energy levels.

:::note Who this is for
Sellers on WhatsApp, Instagram, TikTok or their own store, whether you sell part-time or full-time. If you are just starting, skip the review section and go straight to goals.
:::

## Step 1: review your year honestly

Before you plan forward, look back. Gather your order records, bank statements, WhatsApp chats and any notes. If your orders are in an app, download or view your sales summary.

### The numbers to look at

You do not need complex accounting. Find these:

- **Total sales** for the year, or as close as you can estimate
- **Number of orders**
- **Average order value:** total sales divided by number of orders
- **Your best-selling products,** the top five
- **Your best months** and your slowest months
- **Rough profit:** sales minus stock, delivery, packaging, ads and fees

### The questions to answer

Numbers tell you what happened. These questions tell you why:

1. Where did most of my customers come from? WhatsApp Status, Instagram, TikTok, referrals or ads?
2. Which products made money, and which just kept me busy?
3. What problems kept coming back? Late deliveries, slow replies, stock running out?
4. How many customers bought more than once?
5. What did I enjoy, and what drained me?

:::checklist Year-in-review checklist
Total sales and number of orders
Average order value
Top five products by sales
Best and slowest months
Rough profit for the year
Main sources of customers
Three biggest problems
Number of repeat customers
:::

### A worked example

Here is an example seller, Amaka, who sells skincare from Ibadan. In her review, she finds:

- Total sales: ₦3,600,000 from 450 orders
- Average order value: ₦8,000
- Top product: shea butter body cream, almost a third of her sales
- Best months: November and December
- Main problem: running out of her best product in December
- Most customers came from WhatsApp Status and referrals

That one page already tells her a lot. She should protect her best product's stock, grow referrals, and try to raise her average order value.

## Step 2: set three goals for 2027

Now pick three goals. Not ten. Three is enough to make real progress without spreading yourself thin.

### Make each goal clear and measurable

"Grow my business" is a wish. "Increase monthly sales from ₦300,000 to ₦450,000 by June" is a goal. Each goal should say **what, how much and by when.**

### Three types of goals to consider

Try to pick one from each of these areas:

- **Sales goal:** more revenue, more orders or a higher average order value.
- **Customer goal:** more repeat customers, more referrals or a new channel like TikTok.
- **Operations goal:** fewer late deliveries, faster replies, better stock control or more time off.

### Amaka's three goals

1. **Sales:** raise average order value from ₦8,000 to ₦10,000 by 31 March, using bundles.
2. **Customers:** get 50 repeat orders a month by June, using WhatsApp follow-ups.
3. **Operations:** never run out of her best product, by keeping four weeks of stock at all times.

:::tip Keep at least one goal about your wellbeing
A business that burns you out is not sustainable. A goal like "close orders by 9pm" or "take Sundays off" is as valid as any sales target.
:::

## Step 3: build your 90-day plan

A year is too long to plan in detail. Ninety days is long enough to see results and short enough to stay focused. Plan January to March now, then plan the next 90 days at the end of March.

### Turn each goal into actions

For each goal, list the actions that will get you there. Then put those actions into weeks.

For Amaka's average order value goal, actions might include:

- Create three bundles at ₦10,000, ₦15,000 and ₦20,000
- Offer free delivery above ₦12,000
- Add a "complete the set" suggestion when confirming orders

### Break the 90 days into three months

:::steps A 90-day rhythm
Month 1: set up | Build bundles, write message templates, fix stock tracking and set your weekly routine.
Month 2: push | Run your actions consistently, try one new channel or ad test, and track results weekly.
Month 3: improve | Drop what is not working, double down on what is, and draft your next 90-day plan.
:::

## Step 5: review and adjust every week

A plan only works if you look at it. Set a fixed time each week, for example Sunday evening, for a 20-minute check-in.

### Your weekly check-in questions

1. Did I do my weekly habits for each goal?
2. What are the numbers this week compared with last week?
3. What got in the way?
4. What is one thing I will do differently next week?

### Monthly reset

At the end of each month, look at the bigger picture. Are you on track for your 31 March targets? If a goal is clearly too easy or too hard, adjust it. Changing a goal based on real information is good planning, not failure.

### Plan for the predictable

Some things you know will happen in the first quarter: January is often slow after December spending, and Valentine's Day is a gifting moment in February. Build these into your plan. A January clearance sale and a February gift bundle are easy wins to put on the calendar now.

## Common planning mistakes to avoid

Even a simple plan can go off track. These are the mistakes many sellers make in January, and how to avoid them.

### Setting goals with no starting number

If you do not know where you are, you cannot tell if you are improving. Even a rough starting number, like "about 30 orders a month", is better than nothing. Write it down before you set the target.

### Copying someone else's targets

A seller on TikTok may say they made ₦2 million in a month. Their products, prices, audience and costs are different from yours. Build your targets from your own numbers.

### Planning only for sales

More orders with the same messy process means more stress, more mistakes and more refunds. Make sure at least one goal improves how you work, such as faster replies or better stock tracking.

### Forgetting cash flow

Growth often needs cash before it pays back: more stock, more packaging, maybe ads. Plan when money comes in and goes out. On Shopinbos, each payment settles straight to your bank, usually the next working day, which helps you plan restocks.

### Never writing it down

A plan in your head changes every day. A plan on paper or in your phone holds you to what you decided when you were calm and thinking clearly.
