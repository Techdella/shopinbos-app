If you are wondering **how to start an online business in Nigeria with little capital**, here is the honest answer: pick one product people already buy, avoid buying a big load of stock by dropshipping or buying small, sell from your phone on WhatsApp and Instagram, take payment safely, and put your early profit back into stock. Your biggest costs at the start are data, a few test products and your time.

Plenty of Nigerian sellers begin this way, with a phone and a few thousand naira, and grow step by step. This guide gives you a realistic plan, a sample budget and the mistakes to avoid so your small capital lasts long enough for you to find what sells.

## What "little capital" really means

"Little capital" means different things to different people. For this guide, think of three starting points:

- **Almost nothing:** you have a phone and data, but no money for stock.
- **Small:** you have something like ₦20,000 to ₦50,000 to test products.
- **Modest:** you have ₦100,000 to ₦200,000 and want to use it carefully.

The plan works for all three. What changes is how much stock you hold yourself and how fast you can test.

### Costs you cannot avoid

Even the leanest business has a few costs:

- A smartphone that takes clear photos
- Data for posting, replying and watching what sells
- Delivery costs, which you usually pass to the customer
- Packaging, even if it is simple branded nylon and a thank-you card

### Costs you can delay

Many beginners spend early money on things that can wait:

- A paid website before you have sales
- A big logo design and printed banners
- Bulk stock in many colours and sizes
- Paid ads before you know which product sells

Delay these until you have proof that people are buying.

## Step 1: choose a business you can start small

The best low-capital businesses share a few traits. The product is light and easy to deliver, people buy it again or buy it often, and you can show it well in photos and short videos.

Ideas that suit small budgets:

- **Fashion accessories:** bags, jewellery, scarves, caps, sunglasses
- **Beauty:** lashes, nails, skincare, perfume oils, hair accessories
- **Phone accessories:** cases, chargers, cables, earbuds, screen guards
- **Food and snacks:** chin chin, plantain chips, small chops, zobo, from your own kitchen
- **Home items:** kitchen gadgets, organisers, scented candles, throw pillows
- **Services sold online:** braiding, makeup, tailoring, graphic design

:::tip Sell what you understand
If you already know a market, like fashion in Aba or gadgets in Computer Village, start there. Your knowledge is capital too, and it saves you from expensive mistakes.
:::

## Step 2: get stock without spending all your money

Stock is where most capital disappears. There are three ways to keep it small.

### Option A: dropship (no stock)

With dropshipping, you list products from a supplier, a customer orders from you, and the supplier ships the item. On Shopinbos, you can [dropship from checked Nigerian suppliers](/features/dropshipping/) who ship with your shop's label, and you are paid after delivery. You do not buy anything upfront.

The trade-off: your profit per item is usually lower, and you depend on the supplier's stock and speed. It is a good way to test products before you commit money.

### Option B: buy small quantities

Buy two or three pieces of a few products and see what moves. Markets like Balogun, Trade Fair, Aba and Kano let you buy in small quantities, though prices are better in bulk. Many sellers start with ₦20,000 worth of stock spread across five or six items.

### Option C: pre-orders

Post the product, collect orders and payment first, then buy from the supplier. This works well for fashion and custom items, but be honest about the waiting time and never take money for something you cannot deliver.

You can compare suppliers and prices on the [Shopinbos marketplace](/marketplace/) before you decide which option suits each product.

## Step 3: work out your numbers before you sell

Small capital means every naira must work. Before you post a single product, know your costs and your profit.

Here is an example for a fictional seller of ladies' handbags:

- Cost from supplier: ₦9,000
- Packaging: ₦300
- Payment fee if paid online: ₦325 (1.5% + ₦100 on ₦15,000)
- Your selling price: ₦15,000
- Profit before your time and data: about ₦5,375

Delivery is shown to the customer at checkout, so it does not come out of your margin. If you offer free delivery, add it to your costs.

:::steps Price a product in five minutes

Set your price | Choose a price that covers costs and leaves a real profit, not just ₦500.
Test it | Post at that price and watch how buyers react.
Adjust | If many ask but few buy, check the price, photos or trust signals.
:::

## Step 4: set up where you will sell

You do not need a costly website to start. You need a place where buyers can see your products and a simple way to order.

### Start with the channels you already use

- **WhatsApp Business:** set up your profile, catalogue and Status. Your contacts are your first customers.
- **Instagram:** a clean business page with real product photos and clear prices.
- **TikTok:** short videos showing the product in use can reach people far beyond your contacts.
- **Facebook groups and Marketplace:** good for local buyers in your city.

## Step 5: get your first 10 customers for free

Your first customers usually come from people who already know you. That is fine. They are your testers and your first reviews.

:::checklist First customer plan
Post your products on WhatsApp Status every day for two weeks
Tell friends, family and colleagues directly, with a link
Ask your first buyers for a photo or short review
Join two or three relevant Facebook or WhatsApp groups and follow their rules
Post at least three short videos a week on TikTok or Instagram Reels
Reply to every enquiry within an hour where you can
:::

### Let your content do the talking

You do not need a big budget for marketing. Simple, honest content works: a short video of you packing an order, a close-up of the product in daylight, a customer's kind message. Post what you have, even if it is not perfect. Many sellers find their plain, real videos get more questions than polished flyers, because buyers can see a real person and a real product.

### Use reviews as your marketing

Every happy customer is free advertising. Screenshot kind messages (hide their names and numbers) and post them on Status and in an Instagram highlight. New buyers trust other buyers more than they trust your captions.

## Step 6: take payment and deliver without losing money

With small capital, a single unpaid delivery or a fake alert can wipe out a week's profit. Protect yourself.

### Payment

- Confirm money is in your account or dashboard before you dispatch. Never rely on screenshots.
- Use payment links so you are not matching bank alerts to chats.
- If you offer pay on delivery, use a system that protects you. Shopinbos pay on delivery covers orders up to ₦50,000 in Lagos, Abuja, Ibadan and Port Harcourt, with a 4-digit code the customer gives the rider, and riders pay the cash to you by 6pm the same day.

### Delivery

- Book riders for local orders and tell the customer the delivery fee before they pay.
- For other states, use delivery partner companies and share tracking where you can.
- Pack neatly. Good packaging makes a ₦5,000 product feel like a ₦10,000 one.

:::warn Do not mix business and personal money
Open a separate account for the business, even a simple one. When everything sits in one account, it is easy to spend your stock money without noticing.
:::

## Step 7: grow from profit, not loans

Once sales come in, the temptation is to spend. Instead, set simple rules:

1. Put back the cost of stock first, every time you sell.
2. Split profit: some for you, some to grow the business.
3. Restock what sells, drop what does not.
4. Add one new product or category at a time.
5. Only try paid ads on products that already sell well without them.

## Mistakes that eat small capital

- **Buying too much of one product.** Test first, then buy more.
- **Copying a trend too late.** By the time everyone sells it, prices drop. Watch what is rising, not what has peaked.
- **Ignoring delivery costs.** If the customer refuses to pay delivery, you might be paying it twice.
- **Giving credit to friends.** "I'll pay you month end" can quietly drain your stock money.
- **Quitting too early.** The first few weeks are often slow. Keep posting, keep improving and track what people ask about.
