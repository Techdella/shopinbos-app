The best Detty December business ideas are the ones that serve people who are **going out, dressing up, hosting and travelling**: party outfits, hair and beauty, small chops and drinks, gifts, rentals and event extras. December in Lagos, Abuja, Port Harcourt and other cities brings concerts, weddings, owambes and visitors home from abroad, and many of them want things fast.

But a busy December can also go wrong quickly. Riders get booked out, cash goes missing, stock runs out on the 23rd and you are too tired to reply to messages. This guide gives you festive business ideas you can still act on, and then covers the four things that decide whether you make money: delivery cut-offs, cash, extra hands and stock.

## What makes Detty December different

For most of the year, your customers are people at home and at work. In December, a large share of them are in party mode. Many people from the diaspora visit, families gather, and there is an event almost every night in the big cities.

That changes what people buy and how:

- **They buy for a specific date.** "I need this for Saturday's concert."
- **They want it quickly,** often same day or next day.
- **They pay more for convenience,** such as delivery to a hotel or short-let.
- **They buy on impulse** after seeing something on Status or TikTok.

If you can deliver on time in your city, December can be your best month.

## Detty December business ideas you can still start

These ideas work for online sellers who already have some stock, skills or supplier contacts. Most can be set up in a few days.

### Party and concert outfits

Two-piece sets, sequin dresses, shirts, sneakers, caps and bags for nights out. Post outfits styled for specific events: "Owambe ready", "Concert fit", "Beach day". Fashion sellers can find more ideas in our [fashion selling guide](/solutions/fashion/).

### Hair, beauty and grooming

Wigs, lashes, nails, perfume oils, body glow and grooming kits for men. People want to look good in photos. Bundles like "Night out kit" with lashes, lip gloss and a setting spray sell well. See our [beauty selling guide](/solutions/beauty/) for more.

### Small chops, cakes and drinks

House parties and family gatherings need food. Small chops trays, cakes, puff puff, chapman and zobo in bottles are December favourites. Be honest about how many you can make in a day, and take orders with a clear cut-off. Our [food selling guide](/solutions/food/) has tips on packaging and delivery.

### Gifts for returning visitors and hosts

Visitors want gifts to take back, and hosts want small thank-you gifts. Think Ankara items, local snacks like cashew and plantain chips, shea butter, beads and branded tote bags.

### Rentals and event extras

Party lights, cooler boxes, speakers, chairs, tableware and photo props. If you own items that sit unused, renting them out for a weekend can bring extra income. Agree on deposits and return dates in writing.

### Beach and travel essentials

Swimwear, sunglasses, sandals, travel-size toiletries, power banks and phone pouches. People heading to beaches and travelling between cities buy these at the last minute.

:::tip Use what you already sell
You do not need a brand new business for December. Re-package what you already stock with a festive angle. A perfume oil seller can sell "Owambe scent kits". A sneaker seller can sell "Concert fit" combos.
:::

## Plan your delivery cut-offs

Delivery is where December goes wrong most often. Roads are busy, riders are stretched, and many delivery companies slow down or pause around Christmas and New Year.

### Set your own last order dates

Ask your riders and interstate delivery partners for their last reliable dispatch days and holiday closures. Then set your own cut-offs a day or two earlier, and post them everywhere: your Status, your bio, your store banner and your auto-reply.

A typical setup might look like this (use your partners' real dates):

| Order type | Example cut-off |
|---|---|
| Interstate delivery before Christmas | Mid-December |
| Same-city delivery before Christmas | 22 or 23 December |
| Same-city delivery before New Year | 30 December |
| Pickup from you | Up to the day itself, by appointment |

### Book riders early and in batches

Do not wait until an order is ready to look for a rider. On Shopinbos, you can [book riders from the order](/features/logistics/), and the delivery price is shown at checkout so customers know the cost upfront. Group orders by area and send them together to save time and money.

### Offer pickup and same-day windows

Pickup points, a fixed pickup time at your shop or a public place, and "order by 12pm for same-day delivery" all help you manage the rush. Clear windows reduce "where is my order?" messages.

:::warn Do not promise exact times on peak days
On days like 24 and 31 December, traffic and demand are unpredictable. Give delivery windows, such as "between 2pm and 6pm", rather than exact times.
:::

## Get extra hands, and set them up well

If orders double, you cannot pack, post, reply and deliver alone. Many sellers bring in a sibling, a friend or a part-time helper for December.

### Split the work

Give each person one clear job:

- **Replies and order taking**
- **Packing and labelling**
- **Rider booking and tracking**
- **Content: photos, videos and Status**

### Write down the basics

Before your helper starts, write a short guide: your prices, delivery areas and costs, cut-off dates, how to confirm payment and what to say when something goes wrong. This avoids mistakes and saves you from answering the same questions all day.

### Let AI take the repeat questions

Many December messages are the same: "How much?", "Do you deliver to Lekki?", "Is it available in size 42?" An assistant like [Peggy](/features/peggy-ai/) can answer these in English or Pidgin and draft your posts, so your helpers can focus on packing and delivery.

:::steps Daily routine for a December team
Morning check | Review overnight orders, confirm payments and update stock.
Midday packing | Pack all orders for the afternoon delivery run in one go.
Rider booking | Book riders by area and send customers their delivery window.
Evening content | Post new arrivals, cut-off reminders and customer photos.
Close-out | Record cash, check pay on delivery codes and plan tomorrow.
:::

## Plan your stock so you do not run out

Running out on 23 December is painful. So is being left with a room full of party wear on 2 January.

### Know your best-sellers

Look at what sold most in the last two months. These are the items to stock deeper. New, untested items should be ordered in small quantities or dropshipped.

### Talk to suppliers about their holidays

Many suppliers and markets close for some days around Christmas and New Year. Ask your suppliers when they will close and when they reopen, and order before then. You can also [find checked suppliers](/marketplace/) who offer dropshipping, so you can keep selling without holding extra stock.

### Have a plan for leftovers

Decide now what you will do with stock left after the holidays: a January sale, bundles with slower items, or keeping them for Valentine's Day.

## Price for the festive rush without scaring people off

December costs more to run. Riders may charge more on busy days, packaging runs out, and you may be paying a helper. If you do not adjust, a busy month can still leave you with little profit.

### Add a festive delivery or rush fee

It is fair to charge more for same-day or late-evening delivery in December, as long as you say so upfront. Show the delivery price at checkout so nobody is surprised. Customers who need something for tonight's party usually accept a rush fee.

### Use bundles instead of discounts

Rather than cutting prices, add value. "Outfit plus bag for ₦32,000" or "Small chops tray plus 6 bottles of zobo" makes the order bigger and feels like a deal, while protecting your margin.

### A quick example

Say an example seller sells a party dress for ₦18,000 that costs her ₦11,000. Packaging is ₦500, and the payment fee on ₦18,000 is ₦370. Her profit is ₦6,130. If she pays a helper ₦3,000 a day and sells five dresses a day, the helper costs ₦600 per dress, so she still keeps over ₦5,500 per dress. Run this simple maths for your own products before the rush peaks.

:::tip Price for your time too
If a custom order takes you three hours, charge for those hours. December is when your time is most valuable.
:::

## After the rush: what to do in January

When the parties end, do three things:

1. **Thank your customers.** A short WhatsApp message keeps you in mind for next time.
2. **Count your numbers.** Revenue, profit, best-sellers and what went wrong.
3. **Rest.** Plan a few quiet days before you start planning the new year.
