If you want to know how to get repeat customers, start with this: **make the first order easy, follow up after delivery, and give people a reason to come back.** Most repeat business does not come from clever marketing. It comes from a good experience and a seller who remembers the customer.

For online sellers in Nigeria, WhatsApp is where most of this happens. A short thank-you message, a check-in when the product might run out, and a small reward for the third order can turn a one-time buyer into someone who orders every month. This guide shows you how, step by step.

## Why repeat customers matter more than new ones

Getting a new customer takes effort. They have to find you, trust you, ask questions, and take a chance on a seller they have never met. A returning customer already knows you deliver, already has your number and already likes what you sell.

That is why many sellers find that a small group of loyal customers brings in a big share of their sales. They also tell friends, share your posts on their Status and forgive the odd mistake. When ad costs go up or the naira moves, these customers keep your business steady.

**Think of it this way.** If you spend ₦2,000 on ads to get one new customer who spends ₦10,000 once, that is one sale. If that same customer comes back four more times without any extra ad spend, you have earned five sales from one ₦2,000. These figures are an example, but the logic holds for most sellers.

## Start with a first order worth remembering

You cannot follow up your way out of a bad first experience. Before you work on loyalty, check that the basics are solid.

### Deliver what you showed

The product should look like the photo. If the colour is slightly different in real life, say so in the description. Surprises are the fastest way to lose a customer.

### Deliver when you said

Give an honest delivery window and keep it. If something goes wrong, message the customer before they message you. A seller who says "your rider is delayed by an hour, sorry" is trusted more than one who goes quiet.

### Pack it well

Clean packaging, a small thank-you card and your WhatsApp number on a sticker all help. The card does not need to be expensive. A handwritten "Thank you, Ada" goes a long way.

:::checklist First-order basics
Product matches the photos and description
Delivery window given and kept
Neat, secure packaging
Thank-you card with your name and number
Easy way to pay, including pay on delivery where possible
Fast, polite replies to questions
:::

## How to follow up on WhatsApp after a sale

This is where most sellers stop, and where you can stand out. A follow-up is not spam if it is short, personal and useful.

### The day-of-delivery message

Send this a few hours after the order arrives:

> "Hi Tolu, I hope your bag arrived safely. Thank you for buying from us. If anything is not right, just reply here and I will sort it."

That one message does two things. It catches problems early, and it shows you care after you have been paid.

### The review or photo request

Two or three days later, ask for feedback:

> "Are you enjoying the bag? If you have a minute, I would love a photo or a short review. It really helps a small business like mine."

Customer photos make excellent Status posts. Always ask before you share them.

### The "running low" reminder

If you sell things people use up, such as skincare, hair products, snacks, spices or perfume oils, you know roughly when they will run out. Send a reminder just before:

> "Hi Funke, your body butter should be getting low by now. Want me to send another one this week? Same price, same address."

This is one of the most effective repeat-sale messages because it is helpful, not pushy.

:::steps A simple follow-up timeline
Day 0 | Confirm the order and give a delivery window.
Delivery day | Check it arrived well and thank them.
Day 3 | Ask for a review or photo.
Day 21 to 30 | Send a reminder or a related product, depending on what they bought.
Every 4 to 6 weeks | Share one new arrival or offer that matches their taste.
:::

:::warn Do not add people to broadcasts without asking
Ask customers if they want to hear about new stock. Use WhatsApp broadcast lists only for people who have saved your number and agreed. Unwanted messages get you blocked and reported.
:::

## Keep track of your customers

You cannot follow up with people you have forgotten. As orders grow, your memory will not keep up.

### What to record for each customer

- Name and phone number
- What they bought and when
- Their delivery area
- Sizes, shades or preferences
- Any special dates they mentioned, like a birthday

A notebook works at first. A spreadsheet is better. An order system that saves this for you is best, because it is there when you need it. ### Group your customers

Once you have a list, sort people into groups: first-time buyers, people who have bought two or more times, and people who have not bought in three months. Each group gets a different message.

- **First-time buyers:** thank them, ask for feedback, suggest one related item.
- **Regulars:** early access to new stock, a small reward.
- **Quiet customers:** a friendly "we miss you" with something new.

## Simple loyalty ideas that work for small sellers

You do not need a points app. Simple, clear rewards are easier to run and easier for customers to understand.

### Stamp card on WhatsApp

"Buy 5 times, get the 6th delivery free." Keep a note of each customer's count and tell them where they are: "This is your 3rd order, two more and your next delivery is on us."

### Free gift on the third order

A small extra, like a mini product sample, a hair tie or a pack of chin chin, makes the customer feel noticed. It costs you little and builds goodwill.

### Early access for regulars

Message your best customers before you post new stock publicly. "You are one of my regulars, so you are seeing this first." People like being on the inside.

### Referral reward

Offer a discount to both the customer and the friend they bring. For example, ₦1,000 off each when a friend orders using their name.

:::tip Keep the reward smaller than your profit
Work out your margin first. If you make ₦3,000 profit on an order, a ₦500 reward is fine. A ₦3,000 reward means you worked for free.
:::

## Make reordering effortless

The easier it is to buy again, the more people do it.

- **Save their address and preferences** so they do not repeat themselves.
- **Send one link** to your store or catalogue rather than ten product photos. Our guide on [selling on WhatsApp](/features/sell-on-whatsapp/) covers how to share a catalogue that people can order from.
- **Offer the same payment options as last time.** If they paid on delivery before, let them do it again. Card, bank transfer and USSD should all be available.
- **Reply fast.** A returning customer who waits hours may buy elsewhere. If you are busy, an assistant like [Peggy](/features/peggy-ai/) can answer common questions in English or Pidgin.

## Handle problems in a way that wins loyalty

Mistakes happen: wrong size, late rider, damaged item. How you respond matters more than the mistake.

1. **Reply quickly and calmly.** Do not argue in the chat.
2. **Say sorry and own it,** even if the rider caused it.
3. **Offer a clear fix:** exchange, refund or a discount on the next order.
4. **Follow up** after the fix to check they are happy.

Many sellers find that a customer whose problem was solved well becomes more loyal than one who never had a problem. They have seen you behave well under pressure.

## Repeat-customer messages you can copy

Here are a few more short messages you can adapt. Change the names, products and prices so they sound like you.

### New arrival for a past buyer

> "Hi Kemi, we just got new ankle boots in the same leather as the bag you bought in October. I kept a pair in your size aside until tomorrow evening. Want me to send a photo?"

This works because it is specific. It shows you remember what she bought and her size.

### Birthday or special date

> "Happy birthday, Musa! Thank you for buying from us this year. Here is ₦1,000 off your next order this month, just mention BDAY when you order."

Only send this if the customer told you their birthday. It feels warm, not creepy, when they shared it with you.

### "We miss you" message

> "Hi Grace, it has been a while! We now deliver to Ajah on the same day, and we have a new body oil I think you would like. Shall I send the details?"

Give a real reason to come back, such as faster delivery, a new product or a payment option they asked for before.

**Write like you talk.** Short, friendly messages with the customer's name get far more replies than long, formal broadcasts. A little Pidgin is fine if that is how you normally chat with them.

## Measure whether it is working

Once a month, look at two simple numbers:

- **How many orders came from returning customers?**
- **How many customers bought more than once?**

You do not need a fancy dashboard. Count them from your order list. If the number goes up after you start following up, keep going. If it does not, look at which messages get replies and which are ignored, and adjust.

:::checklist Monthly repeat-customer review
Count orders from returning customers
List your top 10 customers by number of orders
Message anyone who has not ordered in 90 days
Check which follow-up messages got replies
Plan next month's reward or early-access offer
:::
