If you are wondering how to find reliable suppliers in Nigeria, the short answer is: **start in the market that specialises in your product, buy small first, check the supplier before you pay a large amount, and keep records of everything.** Reliable suppliers are rarely the cheapest on day one. They are the ones who deliver the same quality, at the agreed price, every time.

This guide covers where to look, from Aba to Kano to Balogun and Computer Village, what to check before any money moves, how to spot a scam, and how to build a supplier relationship that lasts. It is written for online sellers who buy to resell on WhatsApp, Instagram or their own store.

## What "reliable" actually means

Before you go searching, be clear on what you need. A reliable supplier:

- **Delivers the quality you agreed,** not a cheaper version
- **Keeps the price** you agreed, or tells you early when it changes
- **Restocks** in a time you can plan around
- **Communicates** clearly and answers when there is a problem
- **Handles faults fairly,** with a clear exchange or refund process

A supplier who is 10% cheaper but sends wrong sizes half the time will cost you more in refunds, angry customers and wasted delivery fees.

## Where to find suppliers in Nigeria: the main markets

Nigeria has well-known markets for almost every product category. Going in person, or sending someone you trust, is still one of the best ways to see quality and meet sellers face to face. Below are some of the markets online sellers often mention. Prices and availability change, so always compare.

### Aba: clothing, shoes and bags

Aba in Abia State is known for local manufacturing of shoes, bags, belts and clothing, with Ariaria Market as a major hub. It is a strong option if you want made-in-Nigeria fashion items and the possibility of custom orders with your own designs or labels.

**Good for:** leather slides, sandals, bags, shirts, school and work shoes, custom branding.

### Kano: textiles, grains and bulk goods

Kano is a long-standing trade centre for the North. Kantin Kwari is well known for fabrics and textiles, and the city is also a source for grains, spices, dried goods and other bulk produce.

**Good for:** fabrics, kaftans and native wear materials, spices, dried food items in bulk.

### Balogun and Lagos Island: fashion and general goods

Balogun Market on Lagos Island is a busy hub for fabrics, ready-to-wear clothing, shoes, bags, jewellery and accessories. Nearby areas like Idumota and Oke Arin are known for general goods and provisions.

**Good for:** Ankara and lace, ready-to-wear, accessories, provisions and packaged foods.

### Computer Village: phones and gadgets

Computer Village in Ikeja, Lagos is the best-known place for phones, laptops, chargers, earbuds and accessories. Quality ranges from brand new to refurbished to fake, so checks matter even more here.

**Good for:** phone accessories, power banks, earbuds, chargers, cases. For more on this category, see our [electronics selling guide](/solutions/electronics/).

### Other markets worth knowing

- **Onitsha Main Market** in Anambra: a very large market for many categories.
- **Alaba International** in Lagos: electronics and home appliances.
- **Trade Fair Complex** on the Lagos-Badagry Expressway: a wide mix of goods.

:::tip Visit on a quieter day if you can
Big markets are crowded, especially in December. A quieter weekday lets you talk properly with sellers, compare quality and take notes without pressure.
:::

## Checks to do before you pay any supplier

This is the most important part of the guide. Do these checks before you send a large amount of money, no matter how friendly or convincing the supplier seems.

:::checklist Supplier checks before paying
Get their full name, business name, phone number and physical shop address
Search the business name on the CAC public search to see if it is registered
Ask for photos or a short live video of the actual stock, not catalogue images
Ask for references from two other sellers who buy from them
Check that the bank account name matches the person or business you are dealing with
Agree price, quantity, quality, delivery date and refund terms in writing on WhatsApp
Order a small sample before a bulk order
:::

### Visit or send someone

If the supplier claims to have a shop, ask to visit or send a trusted friend. Real suppliers usually welcome serious buyers. If they always have a reason why you cannot come, be careful.

### Ask for a live video call

A quick video call where they walk you through the stock is hard to fake. Ask them to show the product from different angles and say today's date.

### Start with a sample

Order one or a few units first. Check quality, packaging, how long delivery took and how they communicated. This small test saves you from big losses.

### Agree everything in writing

A WhatsApp message counts as a record. Write down the price per unit, quantity, colour or size breakdown, delivery date and what happens if items are faulty. Ask them to confirm with a clear "yes."

:::steps A safe first order
Ask questions | Get prices, minimum quantity, delivery time and refund terms.
Verify | Check their address, references and the name on the bank account.
Sample | Buy a small quantity and inspect it carefully.
Small batch | Place a modest order, perhaps enough for two or three weeks.
Review | If quality and delivery were good, increase your next order gradually.
:::

## How to avoid supplier scams

Most suppliers are honest people trying to run a business. But scams do happen, especially online. Watch for these signs.

:::warn Common red flags
Be careful with prices far below everyone else, pressure to pay "today only", or a bank account name that does not match the business. Walk away from anyone who has no address, refuses video calls and wants full payment for a large first order with no sample.
:::

### Classic scam patterns

- **The disappearing supplier:** takes payment, then blocks you. Protect yourself by starting small and verifying identity.
- **The bait and switch:** shows good samples, then sends lower quality in bulk. Inspect bulk orders before you list them, and agree on quality in writing.
- **The fake waybill:** sends a fake dispatch receipt to delay you. Confirm with the delivery company directly using their official contact.
- **The "pay more for customs" story:** asks for extra fees after you have paid. Agree the total cost upfront.

### If something goes wrong

Keep every message, receipt and transfer record. Contact your bank quickly if you suspect fraud, and report it through the right channels. Tell other sellers you trust so they do not fall for the same person. Our post on avoiding payment scams covers more on protecting yourself.

## Questions to ask a new supplier

A short conversation tells you a lot about a supplier. Ask these before your first order, and note how clearly they answer.

1. **What is the minimum order quantity,** and does the price drop at higher quantities?
2. **How long does a restock take** when an item sells out?
3. **Can you send to my customers directly,** or only to me?
4. **What happens if an item arrives damaged or wrong?** Who pays for the return?
5. **How do you pack items** for delivery, especially fragile ones?
6. **Do you close for holidays,** and when?
7. **Which payment methods do you accept,** and do you offer payment after delivery for regular buyers?

Good suppliers answer quickly and specifically. Vague answers like "no problem, everything is fine" to every question are a sign to slow down and check more.

### Market tips when you buy in person

- Go early, before the heat and crowds build up.
- Carry a notebook or use your phone to record prices, shop numbers and contacts.
- Do not buy from the first shop. Compare at least three for quality and price.
- Check items properly: stitching, zips, soles, seals and serial numbers.
- Arrange how goods will get back to you before you pay, whether by your own transport or a trusted carrier.

## Building a long-term relationship with a good supplier

Once you find a reliable supplier, treat the relationship well. Good suppliers prioritise good customers.

- **Pay on time,** as agreed.
- **Give clear orders** with exact quantities, sizes and colours.
- **Share feedback,** both good and bad, politely.
- **Plan ahead.** Tell them early about busy seasons like Christmas or back-to-school so they can prepare stock.
- **Ask about better terms** once you order regularly, such as small discounts or payment after delivery.

### Keep a backup

Even with a great supplier, have a second option for your best-selling products. Shortages, price jumps and holidays happen. A backup means your store keeps selling.

## Comparing suppliers fairly

When you have two or three options, compare them on more than price.

| What to compare | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Price per unit at your usual quantity | Your margin |
| Quality of samples | Returns and reviews |
| Restock time | Whether you run out |
| Communication | How fast problems get solved |
| Fault and refund policy | What happens when things go wrong |
| Delivery cost and time to you | Your total cost and speed |

Score each supplier on these points. The best overall score usually beats the cheapest price.
