If you want to know **how to accept payments online in Nigeria**, the short answer is this: give customers a checkout link where they can pay by card, bank transfer or USSD, and offer pay on delivery for people who don't trust paying first. The money should land in one place, match one order, and reach your bank account on a schedule you can plan around.

Most small sellers start with "send to my account" on WhatsApp. It works for the first few orders. Then you start confirming alerts by hand, missing who paid for what, and losing buyers who wanted to pay by card. This guide walks you through each payment method, what it costs, how to set it up and how to keep your records clean.

## The main ways Nigerian customers pay online

Before you choose a setup, know what your buyers actually use. In Nigeria, most online shoppers pay in one of four ways.

- **Bank transfer.** The most familiar option. The customer sends money from their banking app to an account.
- **Debit card.** Common for buyers who shop on websites and are used to entering card details.
- **USSD.** Useful for customers without data or a banking app. They dial a short code on any phone.
- **Pay on delivery.** The customer pays the rider when the item arrives, by cash or transfer.

You don't have to pick one. Many sellers find that offering two or three options at checkout removes the "abeg, how I go pay?" back-and-forth that kills a sale.

:::note Why options matter
A customer who wants to pay by card and only sees an account number may simply leave. Every extra step between "I want it" and "I've paid" is a chance to lose the order.
:::

## Option 1: Collect payment with a checkout link

A checkout link is a page where the customer sees what they're buying, the total, the delivery price, and a "Pay" button. They choose card, transfer or USSD, and the payment is confirmed automatically.

This is the cleanest way to accept payments online because:

1. **Each payment is tied to an order.** No more scrolling through bank alerts trying to match ₦14,500 to the right customer.
2. **You don't share your personal account number** with strangers on social media.
3. **The customer gets a receipt**, which builds trust for the next order.
4. **You see paid and unpaid orders at a glance**, so you only dispatch what has been paid for.

:::tip Price it in, don't add it on
Instead of adding a "card charge" at the end, which annoys buyers, round your price up slightly to cover the fee. A ₦9,800 item can become ₦10,000 and the fee is covered.
:::

## Option 2: Bank transfer to your account (and how to do it safely)

Plenty of customers still prefer to transfer directly. That's fine, but manual transfers come with two problems: matching payments to orders, and fake alerts.

If you accept direct transfers, protect yourself:

- **Confirm in your banking app, never from a screenshot or SMS alone.** Fake debit alerts and edited screenshots are common.
- **Use a business account in your business name** so customers see a name that matches your shop.
- **Ask for the sender name and order number** in the transfer narration.
- **Don't dispatch until the money shows in your actual balance.**

A better approach is to let customers choose "bank transfer" inside the checkout link. They still pay by transfer, which they're comfortable with, but the payment is confirmed against the order automatically, so you're not checking alerts by hand.

:::warn Fake alert rule
If a buyer is rushing you to dispatch "because I've paid already", slow down. Check your balance. A real customer will wait two minutes.
:::

## Option 3: USSD for customers without data

USSD lets a customer pay by dialling a short code from their bank on any phone, even without internet. This matters for buyers outside big cities, older customers, and anyone whose data has just finished.

When USSD is available at checkout, the customer picks their bank and follows the prompts on their phone. For you, it arrives the same way as a card or transfer payment: matched to the order and added to your balance.

You don't need to set up USSD separately with each bank. It comes with the checkout.

## Option 4: Pay on delivery without the cash headache

Pay on delivery (POD) is how many Nigerians prefer to buy from a shop they haven't used before. It removes the fear of "I paid and nothing came". The risk moves to you: the rider might be stuck with an unwanted item, or cash might go missing.

## When does the money reach your bank?

Getting paid is only half the job. You also need to know when you can spend it, so you can restock.

On Shopinbos, your **balance is paid to your bank every Friday**. If you need money sooner, for example to buy stock at Balogun on a Wednesday, you can **withdraw early any time**.

Plan around it:

- **Use the Friday payout for regular costs** like restocking, packaging and data.
- **Withdraw early when a good deal on stock comes up**, rather than borrowing.
- **Keep a small float** in your account so one slow week doesn't stop you buying stock.

## Keeping records and staying out of trouble

When payments come in through one checkout, your records almost keep themselves. Still, a few habits make life easier when you need a loan, file taxes, or work out whether you're making money.

:::checklist Payment records checklist
Every order has a status: unpaid, paid, delivered or refunded
Payments are matched to orders, not just to bank alerts
You note payment fees as a business cost each month
You record pay on delivery cash separately until it is paid in
You keep business and personal money in different accounts
You save refund records with the reason and date
:::

### Refunds

Sometimes you'll need to give money back: wrong size, item out of stock, a delivery that went wrong. Have a clear return and refund policy, and refund through the same channel the customer paid with where you can. That keeps your records tidy and the customer calm.

## Common mistakes to avoid

A few mistakes cost Nigerian sellers money again and again:

- **Dispatching on a screenshot.** Always confirm the money is in.
- **Hiding fees until the end.** Show the delivery price at checkout and price your products to cover processing fees.
- **Offering only one way to pay.** You lose the buyer who wanted the other option.
- **Mixing personal and business money.** You won't know your real profit.
- **Leaving cash with riders for days.** Same-day cash-in protects you.
