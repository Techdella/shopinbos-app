Facebook ads for small business in Nigeria can work on a small budget if you keep things simple: **one product, one clear offer, a phone-made video, and a budget of about ₦5,000 a day for at least a week** before you judge the results. Most small sellers lose money on ads because they boost random posts, change things every day, or send people to a page where they cannot easily order.

This guide gives you a 14-day plan you can follow, with targeting, creative ideas, the numbers to watch and clear rules for when to stop an ad. It works for ads that show on both Facebook and Instagram, since both run from the same Meta ads tools.

## Before you spend a naira: get these ready

Ads send people to you. If what they land on is confusing, the money is wasted. Fix these first.

### A product that already sells

Do not use ads to test a product nobody has bought. Pick something that already gets orders from your WhatsApp Status or Instagram posts. Ads make a good product sell faster. They rarely rescue a weak one.

### A clear price and offer

"DM for price" loses people on ads. Show the price. If you have an offer, make it simple: free delivery in Lagos, a free gift, or a bundle price.

### A place to order

Send people to either a WhatsApp chat or a product page where they can pay. A [simple online store](/features/storefront/) with the price, photos, delivery cost and a pay button removes a lot of back-and-forth. If you prefer WhatsApp, make sure someone is there to reply quickly.

### Your margin

Know how much profit you make per sale. If you make ₦4,000 per order, you cannot afford to spend ₦6,000 in ads to get each one.

:::checklist Ad-ready checklist
A product with past sales
Clear price shown in the ad
Store link or WhatsApp number that someone answers
Delivery cost and areas stated
Profit per sale worked out
A Facebook Page and Instagram account linked in Meta's ad tools
A payment card that works for online payments
:::

## How the ₦5,000 a day budget works

₦5,000 a day is about ₦35,000 a week, or ₦70,000 over 14 days. That is enough to learn something useful, but not enough to spread across many ads. The key is focus.

- **One campaign** for one product or one small collection
- **Two or three ad versions** at most, so each gets enough views
- **At least 5 to 7 days** before big changes, because the system needs time to learn who responds

**On ad costs.** What you pay per click or per message changes with season, competition and how good your ad is. December, for example, is usually busier. Watch your own numbers rather than relying on what someone else paid.

## Choosing the right campaign goal

In Meta's ad tools, you start by choosing a goal. For small Nigerian sellers, three are most useful.

### Messages (click to WhatsApp)

The ad has a button that opens a WhatsApp chat with you. This suits sellers who close sales in conversation, especially for higher-priced items or products where people ask questions about size and colour.

### Traffic or sales to your store

The ad sends people to your product page. This suits sellers whose store lets customers pay directly. It saves you time, because people can order at 2am without waiting for you.

### Engagement (boosted posts)

Boosting a post gets likes and comments, which can be nice for social proof. But likes are not orders. Use this sparingly, and not as your main sales campaign.

:::tip Start with messages if you are unsure
If most of your sales already happen in WhatsApp chats, start with a click-to-WhatsApp ad. You will see quickly whether people who message actually buy.
:::

## Targeting: who should see your ad

Small budgets do best with targeting that is clear but not too narrow.

### Location

Target where you can actually deliver. If you only deliver in Lagos, do not pay to show ads in Kano. If you deliver nationwide through a delivery partner, start with the cities where most of your past orders came from.

### Age and gender

Use what you know from your past customers. If nearly all your buyers are women aged 22 to 40, start there. If you are not sure, keep it broad and let the results show you.

### Interests or broad audience

You can add interests related to your product, such as fashion, skincare or smartphones. Meta also offers a broad, automated audience option that finds people for you based on who responds. Many sellers find broad targeting works well once the ad itself clearly shows who it is for.

### Retargeting

If you have enough visitors or people who have messaged you, you can show ads to them again. These people already know you, so they are often cheaper to convert. It is a good second step once your first campaign is running.

:::warn Avoid stacking too many filters
If you combine a small city, a narrow age range and three specific interests, your audience may be too small for a ₦5,000 daily budget to work well. Start broader and narrow later.
:::

## Creatives: what to put in the ad

The ad itself does most of the work. On a phone, people scroll fast. You have about two seconds to stop them.

### Use video if you can

Short vertical videos, 10 to 20 seconds, filmed on your phone, often do better than polished graphics. Show the product in use: the bag on a shoulder, the cream on skin, the earbuds in an ear.

### Show the price and the offer early

Put the price on screen in the first few seconds, or in the first line of text. "₦18,500, free delivery in Lagos this week" is clearer than a long story.

### Real people and real packaging

Customers like seeing a real person, a real hand and real packaging. A video of you packing an order and saying "this one is going to Lekki" builds trust.

### Three ad ideas to test

1. **Demo:** the product in use, close-up, with price on screen.
2. **Unboxing:** a customer or you opening the package.
3. **Review:** a screenshot of a real customer message (with their permission and personal details hidden) and the product beside it.

## The 14-day plan

Here is the plan. Stick to it and avoid daily tinkering.

:::steps 14 days on ₦5,000 a day
Days 1 to 2 | Launch one campaign with three ad versions at ₦5,000 a day total, and do not touch it.
Days 3 to 5 | Check clicks or messages, and pause any ad that clearly gets no response while the others do.
Days 6 to 7 | Count actual orders from the ads and work out your cost per order.
Day 8 | Keep the best ad, and add one new version based on what worked.
Days 9 to 13 | Let it run, reply to every message fast, and track orders daily.
Day 14 | Review the full numbers and decide: scale, adjust or stop.
:::

### How to track orders from ads

Ask every new customer how they found you, or use a simple code in the ad like "Mention FB10 for free gift." Write down each order that came from the ad. Without this, you are guessing.

## Numbers to watch, and what they tell you

You do not need to understand every number in the ads dashboard. Focus on these:

- **Cost per message or cost per click:** how much it costs to get someone to take the first step.
- **Messages or visits that turn into orders:** if many people message but few buy, the issue is often price, trust or slow replies, not the ad.
- **Cost per order:** total spend divided by orders from the ad. This is the number that matters most.

### A simple example

Say an example seller spends ₦35,000 in a week and gets 10 orders from the ad. Cost per order is ₦3,500. If profit per order is ₦6,000, she keeps ₦2,500 per order after ads. That is worth continuing. If profit per order were ₦3,000, she would be losing ₦500 on each sale.

## When to stop, adjust or scale

Clear rules stop you from wasting money out of hope.

### Stop the ad when

- It has run for 5 to 7 days and produced **no orders** at all.
- Your cost per order is **higher than your profit per order** after two weeks, even after one round of changes.
- You **cannot handle** the messages or orders, for example during a stock shortage.

### Adjust when

- People click or message but do not buy. Look at your price, delivery cost, reply speed and payment options. Offering [pay on delivery](/features/pay-on-delivery/) can help nervous first-time buyers. On Shopinbos it covers orders up to ₦50,000 in Lagos, Abuja, Ibadan and Port Harcourt.
- One ad version does much better than the others. Turn off the weak ones.

### Scale when

- Cost per order is comfortably below your profit per order for a full week.
- Increase the budget slowly, for example from ₦5,000 to ₦7,000 a day, then wait a few days before increasing again. Sudden big jumps can upset results.

:::tip Do not forget your time
If ads bring 40 messages a day, someone needs to answer them. An assistant like Peggy can handle common questions about price, sizes and delivery in English or Pidgin, so you focus on closing orders.
:::

## Common mistakes Nigerian sellers make with Facebook ads

- **Boosting every post** instead of running one focused campaign.
- **Hiding the price** and asking people to DM.
- **Targeting the whole country** when you deliver only in one city.
- **Changing the ad every day,** so it never settles.
- **Slow replies.** People who message from an ad expect a fast answer.
- **Not tracking orders,** so you cannot tell if ads are paying.
