Dropshipping vs inventory comes down to one trade: **dropshipping protects your cash, holding inventory protects your margin.** With dropshipping, you only pay for a product after a customer orders, but you earn less on each sale. With inventory, you buy stock upfront and earn more per sale, but your money sits on a shelf until things sell.

For most new Nigerian sellers, dropshipping is the safer way to test what sells. Once a product sells steadily, buying it wholesale usually makes more sense. Below, we walk through both models with a naira example, the risks people forget, and the signs that tell you it is time to switch.

## What dropshipping and inventory mean

Before comparing, let us be clear on the two models.

### Dropshipping

You list a supplier's product in your store. When a customer orders and pays, the supplier packs and ships it directly to the customer. You never touch the product. Your profit is the difference between your selling price and the supplier's price.

On Shopinbos, [dropshipping](/features/dropshipping/) works with checked Nigerian suppliers. The supplier ships with your shop's label, so the customer sees your brand, and you are paid after delivery.

### Holding inventory

You buy stock in advance, usually at wholesale prices, and keep it at home, in a shop or in a small store room. When an order comes in, you pack and send it yourself or book a rider. Your profit is the selling price minus what you paid per unit, packaging and your other costs.

:::note A third option many sellers use
You do not have to choose one model for everything. Many sellers hold stock of their two or three best-sellers and dropship the rest of their range. We cover this mix below.
:::

## Dropshipping vs inventory: the quick comparison

| | Dropshipping | Holding inventory |
|---|---|---|
| Money needed to start | Low | Medium to high |
| Profit per sale | Lower | Higher |
| Risk of unsold stock | None | Real |
| Control over packing and speed | Less | Full |
| Space needed | None | Some |
| Time spent per order | Less | More |
| Best for | Testing products, wide range | Proven best-sellers |

Neither is better in every case. The right choice depends on how much cash you have, how sure you are that a product will sell, and how much time you can give to packing.

## A worked naira example

Let us use an example seller, Chioma, who sells women's handbags from Lagos. The numbers are illustrative, so plug in your own.

### Option A: dropship the bag

- Supplier's dropship price: **₦14,000** per bag
- Chioma's selling price: **₦20,000**
- Gross profit per bag: **₦6,000**
- Payment fee (1.5% + ₦100 on card or transfer): **₦400**
- Profit per bag after payment fees: **₦5,600**

Chioma sells 20 bags in a month. Her profit is **₦112,000**. She spent nothing upfront on stock, and she had no bags left over at the end of the month.

### Option B: buy the bag wholesale

- Wholesale price for 30 bags: **₦10,500** each, so **₦315,000** upfront
- Packaging per bag: **₦500**
- Selling price: still **₦20,000**
- Payment fee: **₦400**
- Profit per bag: ₦20,000 − ₦10,500 − ₦500 − ₦400 = **₦8,600**

If she sells 20 bags in the month, her profit on those sales is **₦172,000**. That is ₦60,000 more than dropshipping. But she still has 10 bags on the shelf, which means **₦105,000 of her money is tied up** until they sell.

### What happens if sales are slow

Now imagine a slow month and Chioma sells only 8 bags.

- **Dropshipping:** 8 × ₦5,600 = **₦44,800** profit, nothing at risk.
- **Inventory:** 8 × ₦8,600 = **₦68,800** profit on paper, but 22 bags (₦231,000 at cost) are still waiting to sell. If the style goes out of fashion, she may have to discount them below cost.

This is the real difference. Inventory pays more when you are right about demand. Dropshipping costs you less when you are wrong.

:::tip Work out your break-even quantity
Divide your upfront stock cost by what you take home per sale after packaging and fees. In the example, ₦315,000 ÷ ₦19,100 is about 17 bags. Until she sells 17, Chioma has not even got her own money back. Know this number before you buy.
:::

## The hidden costs of each model

The headline margin does not tell the full story. Here is what sellers often forget.

### Hidden costs of dropshipping

- **Lower margin on every order,** which adds up as you grow.
- **Less control over speed.** If the supplier is slow, the customer blames you.
- **Stock can run out** at the supplier without you knowing, unless the system updates it.
- **Harder to add personal touches** like a handwritten note.

### Hidden costs of holding inventory

- **Cash tied up** that could pay for ads or other products.
- **Unsold stock** that may need discounts.
- **Storage and damage.** Heat, damp and pests ruin some products. Breakables get broken.
- **Your time.** Packing ten orders a day takes hours.
- **Price changes.** When the naira moves, stock you bought earlier may be cheaper or dearer to replace, which makes pricing tricky.

:::warn Watch out for perishables and fast fashion
Food, cosmetics with short shelf lives and trend-driven clothing are the riskiest to hold in large quantities. Start small with these, or dropship them while you learn the demand.
:::

## When dropshipping makes the most sense

Dropshipping is a strong choice if any of these apply:

- You are **just starting** and want to test products without spending your savings.
- You want to offer a **wide range** without filling your room with boxes.
- You are testing a **new category**, for example a fashion seller trying gadgets.
- You **do not have time** to pack and send orders every day.
- Your cash is limited and you would rather spend it on content or ads.

## When holding inventory makes the most sense

Buying stock is often the better move when:

- A product **sells steadily every week**, and you can predict demand.
- The **wholesale discount is large** compared with the dropship price.
- You want **fast, same-day delivery** in your city, which is easier when the stock is with you.
- You want to **customise packaging** or bundle items together.
- You have **space and time** to store and pack.

## When to switch from dropshipping to inventory

The smartest path for many sellers is to start with dropshipping and move best-sellers into stock. Here are clear signs it is time.

:::checklist Signs it is time to buy a product in bulk
It has sold consistently for at least six to eight weeks
You can predict roughly how many you will sell next month
The wholesale price gives you a clearly better margin
You have the cash to buy without stopping other spending
You have a safe, dry place to store it
Customers are asking for faster delivery
:::

### How to make the switch safely

:::steps Moving a product from dropship to stock
Check your sales history | Look at how many units sold each week for the last two months.
Ask for wholesale terms | Request the price for different quantities and the restock time.
Buy a small first batch | Order enough for about three to four weeks of sales, not three months.
Keep the dropship listing as backup | If you run out, you can still fulfil orders while you restock.
Review after one month | Compare profit and leftover stock, then decide on the next batch size.
:::

## When to switch from inventory back to dropshipping

It works the other way too. Consider going back to dropshipping, or stopping a product, when:

- Stock has sat unsold for more than two months.
- You keep discounting to clear it.
- The product needs space you want for faster sellers.
- Your cash is stretched and you need it for something more urgent.

There is no shame in this. Good sellers adjust their range all the time.

## The hybrid model: the best of both

Many experienced sellers settle on a mix:

1. **Hold stock** of your top three to five products, the ones that sell every week.
2. **Dropship** the rest of your range, including new items you are testing.
3. **Review monthly** and move products between the two groups based on sales.

This keeps your cash working, your margins healthy on best-sellers and your store looking full. If you are unsure what to test next, the [trending products page](/trending/) is a useful place to look for ideas.
