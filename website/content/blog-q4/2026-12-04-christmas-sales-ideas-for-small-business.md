The best Christmas sales ideas for small business owners in Nigeria are simple: **sell ready-made gifts, not just products**, make ordering easy on WhatsApp and your store, and tell customers clearly when the last order date is. People are not shopping for themselves in December. They are shopping for a mother in Enugu, a boss in Victoria Island and ten colleagues on one budget.

So your job is to remove the thinking. Put things together, give them a price, show them in a nice photo and say "order by this date and it arrives before Christmas." Below are 14 ideas you can start this week, plus a calendar, pricing examples and the mistakes to avoid.

## Why Christmas selling is different for online sellers

In most months, a customer buys one thing for themselves. In December, the same customer may buy five things for other people, often in a hurry. They care less about the cheapest price and more about three questions: Will it look nice? Will it arrive on time? Can I trust this seller?

That changes how you sell. Your photos should show the gift wrapped or packed, not only the product on a plain background. Your captions should talk about who it is for ("for your mum", "for the office"). And your delivery promise matters more than any discount.

:::note Who this guide is for
Sellers of fashion, beauty, food, gadgets, home items and crafts who sell through WhatsApp, Instagram, TikTok or their own online store. You do not need a big budget for any of these ideas.
:::

## Gift bundle ideas that sell themselves

Bundles are the easiest Christmas win. You take items you already stock, group them, and sell them at one price. The customer gets a complete gift, and you sell more per order.

### 1. "For her" and "for him" bundles

Group three or four items around one person. A beauty seller might do body butter, lip oil and a small mirror in a pouch. A fashion seller might do a cap, socks and a belt. Name the bundle after the person, not the products.

### 2. Price-point bundles

Many buyers start with a budget, not a product. Make three bundles at clear prices, for example **₦10,000, ₦20,000 and ₦35,000**. Put the price in the bundle name so people can choose fast: "The ₦20k Treat Box".

### 3. Office and Secret Santa packs

Offices, churches and friend groups do gift exchanges with a fixed budget. Create a pack that fits a common budget and offer a small discount for 10 or more. One office order can be worth more than a week of single sales.

### 4. Add-on items at checkout

Keep small, cheap items ready to add: a gift card with a handwritten note, a scented candle, a mini chocolate pack. Ask "Do you want to add a card for ₦1,000?" when you confirm the order. Many customers say yes.

:::tip Build bundles from what you already have
Before you buy new stock, look at slow-moving items. A handbag that is not moving alone can sell well inside a "For her" bundle with a purse and a scarf.
:::

## Christmas hamper ideas for Nigerian customers

Hampers are a December favourite, especially for corporate gifts and family visits. They work for food sellers, but also for beauty, home and drinks sellers.

### 5. Food and drinks hampers

Think of what people actually enjoy at Christmas: chin chin, plantain chips, cashew nuts, cookies, juice, tea, coffee, a bottle of wine for those who buy it. Pack them in a basket or a sturdy box with shredded paper. If you sell food, see our [food selling guide](/solutions/food/) for packaging ideas.

### 6. Self-care hampers

Body scrub, shea butter, a towel, a candle and a robe. These are popular with people buying for wives, sisters and friends. A beauty seller can set this up with stock already on the shelf.

### 7. Corporate hampers

Companies buy hampers for staff and clients. They want an invoice, a clear price per unit, a delivery date and the option to add a logo sticker or card. Prepare a one-page price list you can send on WhatsApp in seconds.

:::checklist Before you sell a hamper
Cost of every item, including the basket and wrapping
A photo of the finished hamper, not just the parts
Weight and size, so you can price delivery
Shelf life of any food items
How many you can pack in one day
A last order date
:::

### Pricing a hamper: a worked example

Say your self-care hamper costs you:

- Shea butter and scrub: ₦6,500
- Towel: ₦3,000
- Candle: ₦2,000
- Basket, paper and ribbon: ₦2,500
- Your time to pack: count it as ₦1,000

That is **₦15,000 cost**. If you sell at ₦24,000, you keep ₦9,000 before payment fees. With Paystack, card or transfer payments cost 1.5% + ₦100, capped at ₦2,000, so on ₦24,000 that is ₦460. You still keep about ₦8,540 per hamper. Do this maths for every bundle before you post it.

## Set clear Christmas order deadlines

This is where many small sellers lose money and goodwill. A gift that arrives on 27 December is a problem, not a gift. Customers will forgive "sold out", but they remember "it came late".

### 8. Publish a last order date for each area

Work backwards from 24 December. Ask your rider and your interstate delivery partner when their last reliable dispatch day is, then add a buffer of a day or two. Post something like:

- **Same city delivery:** order by 21 December
- **Interstate delivery:** order by 16 December
- **Custom or personalised items:** order by 12 December

These dates are examples. Use the ones your own delivery partners give you.

### 9. Offer a "too late to post" option

After the cut-off, do not stop selling. Switch to **pickup**, **same-day delivery within your city** or **e-gift vouchers** that the buyer can send on WhatsApp. A voucher with a nice design is still a gift.

:::warn Do not promise what your rider cannot deliver
December traffic in Lagos and Abuja is heavy, and many riders are fully booked. Confirm capacity before you accept a same-day order, especially on 23 and 24 December.
:::

## Make ordering and payment easy

In December, people buy on their phones in between errands. If ordering takes ten messages, you lose them.

## Promote your Christmas range without a big budget

You do not need to spend heavily on ads to have a good December. Use what you already have.

### 13. A 12-day content plan

Post one gift idea a day for 12 days. Day 1 is "Gifts under ₦10k", day 2 is "What to buy your dad", day 3 is a packing video, and so on. Short videos of you wrapping a hamper often get more reactions than polished photos.

:::steps A simple Christmas posting routine
Morning Status | Post one bundle with its price and the last order date.
Afternoon video | Share a 15-second clip of packing or unboxing on TikTok or Reels.
Evening reminder | Remind people how many days are left before the cut-off.
Reply window | Spend 30 minutes answering every message and closing orders.
:::

### 14. Ask past customers first

The people who bought from you in the year already trust you. Send a short, personal WhatsApp message with your Christmas list. Do not blast everyone with the same long broadcast. Group them: people who bought perfume get your scent bundles, people who bought snacks get your hampers.

## A December calendar for small sellers

Here is a simple plan to follow. Adjust it to your city and products.

| Dates | What to do |
|---|---|
| 1 to 5 Dec | Finalise bundles, take photos, set prices, confirm stock with suppliers |
| 6 to 12 Dec | Launch the Christmas collection, message past customers, push corporate hampers |
| 13 to 16 Dec | Remind people about the interstate cut-off, prepare packing materials |
| 17 to 21 Dec | Focus on same-city orders, book riders early, pack in batches |
| 22 to 24 Dec | Pickups, last-minute vouchers, same-day delivery where you can |
| 26 to 31 Dec | Thank-you messages, New Year offers, clear leftover stock |

:::tip Pack in batches, not one by one
Set a packing time each day. Pack all orders for one area together, then book one rider. It saves time and delivery costs.
:::

## Common Christmas selling mistakes

A few mistakes show up every year. Avoid them and you are already ahead.

- **Running out of stock mid-December.** Check with your supplier now how quickly they can restock, and whether they close for the holidays.
- **Discounting too deeply.** People are willing to pay for convenience in December. A small free gift often works better than a big price cut.
- **No clear deadline.** If you do not say it, customers assume they can order on the 24th.
- **Forgetting packaging costs.** Baskets, ribbons and boxes add up. Price them in.
- **Ignoring messages.** A slow reply in December is a lost sale.
