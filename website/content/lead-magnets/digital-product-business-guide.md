# How to Start a Profitable Digital Product Business (Even If You're Not a Creator)

So, you've been thinking about creating something digital to sell, maybe an eBook, an online course or templates, but you're stuck wondering "What do I even sell?" or "Who's going to buy this?"

You're not alone. The good news is, you don't need to be an expert to create digital products people actually want. This guide breaks it down step by step, plus we've got a free toolkit to help you launch within two weeks.

## Why Digital Products Are Perfect for Beginners

More and more Nigerians are paying for online courses, templates and guides, and you can sell to buyers abroad too.

Unlike physical products, you make it once and sell it hundreds of times. No stock to keep, no dispatch riders, no delivery fees.

## Finding Your Profitable Digital Product Idea

Don't overthink this part. You just need to solve a problem people have.

**Look at what you already know:**

- Skills from your job (Excel tips, design basics, CV writing)
- Hobbies you've mastered (Nigerian cooking, fitness routines, hair care)
- Problems you've solved for yourself (budgeting in naira, passing JAMB or professional exams, starting a side hustle)

**Quick test:** Check Facebook groups, Nairaland threads or TikTok comments in your niche. What questions do people ask again and again? That's your product idea.

## Simple Digital Product Ideas That Sell

- **Templates and checklists:** social media templates, budget spreadsheets, meal planners with Nigerian meals
- **Mini-courses:** 30-minute video lessons on one skill
- **eBooks and guides:** step-by-step guides (10–30 pages)
- **Digital tools:** Notion templates, Google Sheets planners, business record books

People pay for convenience and organised information, not just knowledge.

## Free Tools to Create Professional Products

- **For eBooks:** Use [Canva](https://www.canva.com/) or Google Docs, then save as PDF
- **For videos:** Record with your phone or use [Loom](https://www.loom.com/)
- **For templates:** Canva, Google Sheets or PowerPoint

Keep your first product simple. A well-organised PDF guide often sells better than a complicated course.

## Pricing Your Digital Products

Yes, this is the most dreaded part. Here's a simple pricing guide (example ranges for Nigerian buyers):

- **Mini-guides:** ₦2,000–₦5,000
- **Detailed eBooks:** ₦5,000–₦15,000
- **Template bundles:** ₦5,000–₦20,000
- **Mini-courses:** ₦15,000–₦50,000

Price based on value, not how long it took you. Start higher than you think; you can always run a discount later, for example during Black Friday or back-to-school season.

## Best Platforms to Sell

- **[Selar](https://selar.com/):** Built for African creators. Easy setup, naira payments by card, bank transfer and USSD.
- **[Gumroad](https://gumroad.com/):** Simple and popular with international buyers. Prices and fees are in US dollars.
- **[Etsy](https://www.etsy.com/):** Popular for templates and planners. Check first whether you can open a shop and get paid as a Nigerian seller.

You can also sell directly: send a Paystack or Flutterwave payment link on WhatsApp, then deliver the file once payment comes in. Start with Selar or a payment link for zero setup stress.

## Writing [Product Descriptions](https://shopinbos.com/blog/product-description-examples-templates-that-convert/) That Sell

Focus on what the buyer gets, not what you're selling.

Instead of: *"30-page meal planning guide"* Say: *"Never stress about 'what will we eat today?' again. This simple meal plan helps busy families eat well, save money and cut market runs in half. Includes 4 weeks of ready-made Nigerian meal plans plus shopping lists."*

Answer these questions:

1. What problem does this solve?
2. What will their life look like after using this?
3. Why should they trust you to help them?

## Simple Marketing That Works

You don't need thousands of followers. You need the right people to see your work. Try these free tactics:

- **Instagram and TikTok:** Post short previews and tips, with a link in your bio
- **WhatsApp status and broadcasts:** Share tips, reviews from buyers and launch offers with your contacts
- **Facebook groups:** Share helpful tips and mention your product naturally
- **Email list:** Collect emails (Mailchimp or Brevo are free to start) with a free mini-resource
- **LinkedIn posts:** Share lessons, results or quick tips from your work

**The 80/20 rule:** Share 80% helpful content and 20% product promotion.
